# Glossary of Indicators

This glossary summarises common technical indicators and what each one measures. Indicators are calculations derived from price and volume; they summarise data but do not predict the future, and they are best read as context rather than instruction.

**RSI (Relative Strength Index).** A momentum oscillator scaled from 0 to 100 that compares the size of recent gains to recent losses over a chosen lookback, commonly 14 periods. It measures the speed and magnitude of price changes. Conventionally, readings above about 70 are called overbought and below about 30 oversold, though these levels behave differently in trending versus ranging conditions.

**EMA and SMA (Exponential and Simple Moving Averages).** Both smooth price into a running average over a chosen period. An SMA weights every period in the window equally. An EMA weights recent prices more heavily, so it responds faster to new information and lags less. Both are used to describe trend direction and to act as dynamic reference levels.

**VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price).** The average traded price over a session weighted by the volume done at each price. Because it incorporates volume, VWAP is treated as a consensus or benchmark intraday price and typically resets each session.

**ATR (Average True Range).** A volatility measure that averages the true range of price, the greatest of the current bar's high-low span and the gaps from the prior close, over a chosen period. It expresses how much an instrument typically moves per bar and is often used to scale stop distances to conditions.

**ADX (Average Directional Index).** A measure of trend strength scaled from 0 to 100 that ignores direction. Higher values indicate a stronger directional trend, whether up or down, while lower values indicate a weak or absent trend and a more range-like environment.

**Bollinger Bands.** A moving average, typically 20 periods, bracketed by an upper and lower band set a number of standard deviations away, commonly two. Because the bands are built from standard deviation, they widen as volatility rises and narrow as it falls, describing how stretched price is relative to its recent range.

Educational analysis. Not investment advice. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
