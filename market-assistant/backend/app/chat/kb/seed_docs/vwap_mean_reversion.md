# VWAP Mean Reversion

The Volume Weighted Average Price, or VWAP, is the average price at which an instrument has traded over a session, weighted by the volume done at each price. Because it accounts for volume, VWAP is often treated as a reference for the "fair" or consensus intraday price and is watched closely by participants who benchmark their execution against it. It typically resets at the start of each session.

Mean reversion around VWAP is the study of how price behaves when it stretches far from this reference. The observation being examined is that in balanced conditions price tends to oscillate around VWAP: pushes well above it are sometimes met with selling that carries price back toward the line, and pushes well below it are sometimes met with buying. Analysts describe watching for an "extension" or "stretch" away from VWAP, often measured with standard-deviation bands drawn around it, and then studying whether price rotates back toward the average.

This behaviour is most commonly associated with range-bound or balanced sessions, where there is no strong directional conviction and two-sided flow keeps price rotating. In such conditions the distance from VWAP itself can serve as a rough gauge of how stretched the market has become.

The central risk to understand is that mean reversion assumes a range that may not exist. In a strongly trending session, price can ride far above or far below VWAP and stay there, so fading the extension repeatedly works against the dominant flow. For this reason analysts often pair VWAP with a regime check, using tools that distinguish trending from ranging conditions before treating an extension as a reversion opportunity. Watching how price reacts on its first return toward VWAP, and whether volume supports or fades the move, is part of building context rather than a signal in isolation.
