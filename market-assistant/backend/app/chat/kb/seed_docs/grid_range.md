# Grid and Range Trading

Range trading is the study of markets that move sideways between a comparatively stable ceiling and floor rather than trending in one direction. Within such a range, price oscillates: it tends to weaken as it approaches the upper boundary (resistance) and firm as it approaches the lower boundary (support). Analysts identify a range by looking for repeated touches of similar highs and lows and by confirming that no sustained directional structure is present.

The grid concept builds on this by placing a series of staggered reference levels spaced across the range. Rather than treating the range as a single support-to-resistance band, a grid divides it into evenly spaced increments, so that each level marks a zone where behaviour can be observed. The appeal being studied is that a well-defined, choppy range offers many rotations between levels, and a grid provides a structured way to map those rotations. The width of the range and the spacing between grid levels together describe how much room exists between reference points.

Grid and range methods are generally associated with balanced, low-conviction conditions where neither buyers nor sellers dominate. In these conditions the boundaries tend to hold and the oscillation repeats, which is what the framework relies on.

The dominant risk is a breakout. Ranges do not last forever, and when price finally pushes decisively through a boundary the market can transition into a trend. A grid that assumes continued rotation is most exposed at exactly this moment, because the levels that previously turned price no longer hold and positions oriented for reversion sit on the wrong side of a directional move. For this reason range and grid study places heavy emphasis on defining where the range is considered broken, so that the shift from ranging to trending is recognised early rather than fought. A regime filter that distinguishes range-bound from trending conditions is a natural companion to this approach.
