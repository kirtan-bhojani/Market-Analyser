# EMA and VWAP Trend Following

This approach studies trending markets using two complementary tools: a pair of exponential moving averages (EMAs) and the Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP). An EMA is a moving average that weights recent prices more heavily than older ones, so it reacts faster than a simple average. A common configuration pairs a fast EMA, such as a 9-period, with a slower EMA, such as a 21-period. When the fast EMA sits above the slower one and both slope upward, analysts describe the structure as an uptrend; the mirror image describes a downtrend.

VWAP is added as a trend filter. Because VWAP represents the volume-weighted consensus price for the session, price holding above VWAP is often read as broad participation on the buying side, while price holding below it suggests the opposite. Requiring agreement between the EMA structure and the price-versus-VWAP relationship is a way of filtering out ambiguous conditions and focusing study on sessions where direction is comparatively clear.

Within an established trend, the concept being examined is the pullback entry. Rather than reacting to price when it is already extended, analysts watch for a temporary counter-move back toward the fast EMA or toward VWAP, and then study whether the trend resumes from that area. The reasoning is that pulling back to a reference level offers a clearer, better-defined location to assess risk than an extended move does.

The recurring caution in trend following is chasing. When price has already traveled far from its moving averages, entering late means the reference levels that would frame risk are far away, and any normal pullback looks large. Waiting for price to come back to the structure is the discipline this method emphasises. It is also worth noting that trend-following logic underperforms in choppy, sideways markets where EMAs cross repeatedly and VWAP is straddled without follow-through, so a regime check remains valuable.
