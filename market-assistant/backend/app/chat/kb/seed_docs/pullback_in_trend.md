# Pullback Entries Within a Trend

A pullback is a temporary counter-move against the prevailing direction of a trend. In an uptrend it appears as a shallow decline, sometimes called a dip; in a downtrend it appears as a brief rally. The pullback concept studies these pauses as opportunities to assess the trend from a better-defined location rather than reacting to price when it is already extended.

The reasoning starts with market structure. An uptrend is often described as a sequence of higher highs and higher lows, and a downtrend as lower lows and lower highs. A pullback is the portion of that structure where price steps back before, in a continuing trend, making its next push in the trend direction. Because the counter-move retraces toward recent structure, it brings price closer to reference points that make risk easier to frame.

Moving averages are commonly used to locate and gauge pullbacks. Price returning to a rising moving average, such as a 20- or 50-period line, is studied as a possible area where an uptrend may reassert itself, and the mirror applies in a downtrend. Horizontal structure matters too: a prior breakout level, a prior swing low in an uptrend, or a prior swing high in a downtrend can each coincide with a pullback and add context. Analysts also watch the depth of the retracement, since a shallow pullback is often read as trend strength while a very deep one may hint the trend is weakening.

The main caution is distinguishing a pullback from a reversal. A move that begins as a routine dip can continue until the trend structure actually breaks, for example when an uptrend prints a lower low. Because that distinction is only clear in hindsight, this method pairs the structural read with predefined invalidation, so a counter-move that goes too far is recognised as a change in regime rather than a continuation.
